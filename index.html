The case: SpaceX is a tollbooth on the three scarcest things in tech

By Scarcity & Abundance. Every number has a link or is labeled our estimate or scenario.

Our view (thesis). SpaceX is three businesses compounding on each other: compute it rents at scarcity prices, launch nobody else can match on cost, and Starship, which turns both into a much bigger market. Launch is the moat. Compute is where today's price lives. Starship is what makes the long-run numbers possible. This page argues the bull path because that is what the stock has to deliver.

1. Compute: the driver of the price

Company-wide installed GPU power (nameplate compute draw) was 1.7 GW at Sep 30, 2026 (1.4 GW Jun 30; 0.6 GW Sep 30, 2025) (Q3 8-K); it is installed capacity, not actual use, and is not split by site. Q2 AI segment revenue was $2.56B, and Q2 AI capital spending was $15.83B. Source: Q2 10-Q
COLOSSUS and COLOSSUS II together provide about 1.0 gigawatts. COLOSSUS II's first cluster (about 110,000 GB200s, 210 MW) came online in 91 days, the second (110,000 GB300s, 220 MW) in 64 days, and the next phase is expected to add at least 220,000 GB300s and over 400 MW, with no date given. Source: 424B4, p. 77
Anthropic pays $1.25B a month through May 2029 (about $15B a year, contracted, not guaranteed): after an initial three-month period, either side can end it on 90 days’ notice (prospectus p. 14). The capacity covers about 325,000 Nvidia GPUs. Source: 424B4, p. 14 Google pays $920M a month from October through mid-2029, with a delivery grace period ending about Oct 31 and a 90-day exit after Dec 31. Source: Reuters via Web & IT News (press)
What's verified and what isn't (Colossus 2 and Nvidia). Verified in filings: the 1.4 gigawatts company-wide figure, the ~1.0 gigawatts COLOSSUS + COLOSSUS II figure, the cluster sizes above, and $27.96B of non-cancelable commitments, mostly AI infrastructure (10-Q Note 16). Not verified: that Colossus 2 could reach about 1.2 million GPUs by year-end (Musk's Sep 24 X post per CNBC, "if we get lucky"), and that SpaceX is in preliminary talks to debt-finance a large Nvidia GPU purchase (CNBC; Tom's Hardware). The 10-Q never names Nvidia, and no filing shows an Nvidia order. Musk said on the Q2 call SpaceX will use Nvidia chips exclusively and expects a "significant allocation" of Vera Rubin GPUs next year (CNBC, press). We use none of the press GPU counts or dollar figures in the math.
Our view: compute pricing is the whole game. Q3 SI revenue works out to about $17.8B per gigawatt per year (our arithmetic: $7.560B × 4 ÷ 1.7 GW). Excluding $0.54B advertising, SI solutions & infrastructure alone is about $16.5B/GW ($7.022B × 4 ÷ 1.7). Q3 SI includes Cursor (closed Aug 14) and Grok, so this is not pure compute rent. That is now almost equal to consensus, which prices compute at about $17.60 a watt across 4.1 gigawatts, and each +$10/W adds more than $40B of revenue (Morgan Stanley note, Oct 4, via Benzinga). The rate swings more than the build: at 5 gigawatts, a 30% rate cut costs about $11B a year (our arithmetic). The honest caveat: SpaceX buys chips from Nvidia, so Nvidia is paid first; SpaceX earns the spread while compute is scarce. We think scarcity lasts longer than the market fears, because power and build speed are the bottleneck, and SpaceX builds faster than anyone (91 and 64 days per cluster, above).

2. Launch: the real tollbooth

As of Mar 31, 2026, Falcon 9 has reflown a first stage 34 times; the first Falcon 9 (2010) cut launch cost to about $2,700/kg, about 85% below the $18,500/kg historical average (prospectus, citing NASA). Starship aims to cut the cost to orbit by 99% or more; Starship V3 is designed for 100 metric tons fully reusable. Space segment revenue was $0.96B in Q2 2026 and $4.09B in 2025, and the Space segment funded $3.0B of Starship R&D in 2025. Source: 424B4; 10-Q
Our view. Launch is the strongest moat on the page: whoever needs mass in orbit pays SpaceX's price, and Starship widens that gap. Morgan Stanley models about $500/kg by 2030 (TechFlow summary, secondary). Every Starship and AI dollar downstream depends on this cost curve.

3. Starship: five gates, one flywheel

Flight 14 reached orbit on Sep 28, 2026 and deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites (SpaceX). SpaceX expects up to 60 V3s per launch, each designed for 1 Tbps (424B4), about 20x a Falcon 9 load per SpaceX (Space.com). Flights 15 and 16 have no SpaceX-announced date; an FAA planning slide reported by the press lists no earlier than Nov 8 and no earlier than Dec 10 (NSF).
Our view: the cadence-to-revenue flywheel. (1) Reuse lowers cost per kilogram → (2) routine V3 deployment adds about 20x capacity per launch, lifting Starlink → (3) outside customers give Starship its own revenue line → (4) Moon lander milestones bring NASA payments → (5) orbital compute and Mars are the option on top. Each gate opens the next. A failure at gate 1 or 2 delays everything after it, which is why the game locks later gates after a miss.

4. Starlink: the cash engine under it all

13.6M consumer subscribers at Sep 30 (12.0M at Jun 30; 7.2M a year earlier), consumer ARPU $69/month (up from $66 in Q2; $83 a year earlier), Connectivity revenue $5.25B (Q2 $4.29B; Q3 2025 $3.10B). Consumer $3.12B + enterprise & government $2.13B. Source: Q3 8-K (EX-99.1)
Our view. Revenue per subscriber ticked up in Q3 ($66 to $69) while subscribers grew 89% year over year; the test is whether V3 drops cost per bit faster than price over time. Starshield (government) is multi-year revenue recognized over time (10-Q); press reports a $2.29B Space Force data-network award (Breaking Defense, press; official release not pulled).

5. The AI segment beyond rentals: Grok, X, enterprise

Q2 AI revenue: advertising $0.37B (falling) and AI Solutions & Infrastructure $2.19B. Grok-5 is being trained at COLOSSUS II. Q2 AI segment adjusted EBITDA was $1.15B. Source: 10-Q; 424B4
Morgan Stanley's view (press): $300 target = Space $8 + Connectivity $118 + X & Grok $8 + Enterprise AI $165 (sums to $299), with a $600 bull and $75 bear published at initiation (MarketWatch; Saxo). The multiple on AI, not telecom, is most of today's price.

6. Orbital compute, the Moon, Mars, and the Dyson-swarm narrative

SpaceX expects to start deploying orbital AI compute satellites as early as 2028, targets 100 gigawatts a year of compute to orbit (about a million metric tons a year), and writes of propelling humanity toward "Kardashev Type II status." It is collaborating with NASA under Artemis, with Starship as the Human Landing System. Source: 424B4 NASA's auditor says the ship-to-ship propellant transfer test slipped and Artemis III was planned for June 2027, a date since superseded (NASA OIG); Artemis III is now an Earth-orbit docking test, no earlier than late 2027 (Ars Technica). SpaceX shifted priority from Mars to the Moon in February, targeting an uncrewed lunar landing in March 2027 (WSJ, press); Musk wrote that a Mars city would start "in about 5 to 7 years" (RealClearMarkets, press).
Our view. A Dyson swarm of solar-powered compute satellites is the long-dated version of the compute thesis: power is the constraint on Earth and nearly free in orbit. It is pure option value today, with no revenue in any filing horizon. Our 3-year bull case carries it at a $500B scenario value; the base carries $100B.

7. Robots and robotaxis: whose revenue is it?

Optimus is a Tesla product. Musk has said Starship would carry Optimus robots to Mars (Decrypt, Apr 2025), a plan since pushed back by the Moon-first shift. On this page, humanoid robots appear in the Moon and Mars endings as Starship payloads and colony builders: illustration, not SpaceX revenue. Robotaxis accrue to Tesla, not SPCX. The only disclosed ties: Tesla booked $318M of Q2 Megapack revenue from SpaceX and holds a $2.00B (<1%) SpaceX stake (Tesla 10-Q). Neither is in the SPCX price math.

8. Risks, honestly and briefly

  • Compute price reset. Hyperscalers are building their own capacity; for Anthropic, after an initial three-month period, either side can end it on 90 days’ notice (prospectus p. 14). This is the main way the bull case breaks.
  • Starship slips. A gate 1 or 2 failure delays every later revenue line.
  • Capital. About $16.6B a quarter of burn at the Q2 pace against ~$62B of net cash at Jun 30 (downside estimate); Morgan Stanley flagged about $84B a year of external capital 2027-2034 (press).
  • Supply. Oct 22: up to 1.3B; Oct 24: up to 328.4M; Dec 8: up to 328.4M or 797.6M (depends on whether Additional Release Shares went out at the First Earnings Release Date). Total Oct 22–Dec 8: about 1.96B to 2.43B (prospectus pp. 258–259; totals our arithmetic). The Oct 22 and Oct 24 tranches fall in the same week.
  • Legal/power. NAACP Clean Air Act suit over COLOSSUS II turbines (10-Q).

The math behind the four dials

How SpaceX earns money here: it rents GPU capacity it powers and runs. Rental revenue = dial 1 (terrestrial gigawatts billed) × dial 2 (compute price). Value = revenue × dial 3 (multiple). The orbital add-on in the game price uses dial 4 (orbital gigawatts) × dial 2 × dial 3, weighted by your gate 5 pick. Change the dials in the game controls; this window only shows the results.
